Testing, explained

What testing is, and why it matters.

A short, plain-language guide for anyone who has wondered what a tester actually does all day.

Testing is how a team finds out what's true.

Software testing means checking that a product does what it should, and doesn't do what it shouldn't. It isn't only about finding bugs. It gives the team evidence, so decisions about releasing are based on facts instead of hope.

01

Bugs get more expensive over time. A misunderstanding caught in a requirement costs a conversation. The same mistake found by customers costs money, trust and sometimes legal exposure.

02

It protects users. Payments, health data and personal information all depend on software behaving correctly.

03

It's often a legal duty. GDPR expects privacy to be built in and tested. The European Accessibility Act requires many digital services to be accessible.

04

It lets teams move faster. Good automated tests make change safe, so developers can ship with confidence.
